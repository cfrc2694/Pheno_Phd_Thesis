\section{Search strategy and simulation}
\label{sec:strategyandsimulation}


Our proposed search strategy targets three leptoquark production mechanisms (Fig.~\ref{fig:feynmp-prod-channels}): single-$\lq$ ($\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}\to \tau\,\lq$), pair ($\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}\to \lq\,\lq$), and non-resonant ($\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}\to \tau\tau$). At leading $\alpha_s$ order, with $U_1\to \bq\,\tau$ decay focus, distinct topologies emerge:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single-$\lq$ (s$\lq$)}: yields $\textrm{b}\tau\tau$ final state
    \item \textbf{Pair (d$\lq$)}: produces $\textrm{bb}\tau\tau$ signature
    \item \textbf{Non-resonant (non-res)}: generates $\tau\tau$ events
\end{itemize}
Each channel contains two $\tau$ leptons plus 0, 1, or 2 b-jets. Tau decay proceeds either hadronically ($\tau_{\textrm{h}}$) or leptonically into electrons/muons ($\tau_{\ell}$, $\ell = \textrm{e}, \mu$). Our analysis examines six final-state configurations: $\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$, $\bq\,\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$, and $\bq \bq\,\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$, nominally associated with non-res, s$\lq$, and d$\lq$ production respectively.

Experimental reality introduces channel migration effects. Imperfect $\bq$-jet identification or reconstruction causes d$\lq$ signal events to migrate into the $\bq\,\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm h}\tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$ categories. Conversely, soft jets may be misidentified as $\bq$ jets, allowing non-res processes to contaminate the $\bq\,\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$ and $\bq\bq\,\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$ signatures. Our analysis incorporates these signal migration and cross-contamination effects\footnote{Additional signal mixing occurs at the event generation level through higher-order $\alpha_s$ contributions. For instance, in the non-res diagram shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:feynmp-prod-channels}, initial-state $\bq$ quarks can originate from $g\to \bq\bar{\bq}$ splitting, generating non-resonant $\bq\,\tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\mathrm{h}/\ell}$ events. Investigation of such NLO contributions lies beyond this work's scope.}.

Monte Carlo (MC) simulations provide signal and background event estimates. The $U_1$ model from~\cite{Baker:2019sli} was implemented via \texttt{FeynRules} (v2.3.43)~\cite{Christensen:2008py,Alloul:2013bka}, modified to incorporate the Lagrangians specified in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:BasicLagrangian} and~\eqref{eq:BasicLagrangianZp}. Branching fractions and production cross-sections were computed with \texttt{MadGraph5\_aMC} (v3.1.0)~\cite{Alwall:2014bza, Alwall:2014hca}, the latter evaluated at leading $\alpha_s$ order. Sample generation considers $\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}$ collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13 \tev$ and $\sqrt{s}=13.6 \tev$. The NNPDF3.0 NLO~\cite{NNPDF:2014otw} parton distribution function set characterizes all samples, while phase-space optimization employs the full amplitude-squared SDE strategy to accommodate significant $\zb'$ boson interference. Parton-level output interfaces with \texttt{PYTHIA} (v8.2.44)~\cite{Sjostrand:2014zea} for fragmentation and hadronization modeling, followed by \texttt{DELPHES} (v3.4.2)~\cite{deFavereau:2013fsa} detector simulation utilizing CMS geometric configurations for particle reconstruction and identification performance.

Parton-level kinematic requirements impose minimum transverse momentum thresholds: $\pt > 20 \gev$ for jets and leptons, and $\pt > 30 \gev$ for $\bq$ jets. Pseudorapidity acceptance spans $|\eta| < 2.5$ for $\bq$ jets and leptons, extending to $|\eta| < 5.0$ for jets. The production cross-sections presented in Figs.~\ref{fig:branching_ratios} and~\ref{fig:xsinterference} (right panels) reflect these selection criteria.

Tab.~\ref{table:selection_channels} presents analysis-level pre-selection requirements for each channel. Channel categorization relies on $\bq$-jet multiplicity $N(\bq)$, light lepton count $N(\ell)$, hadronic tau count $N(\tau_{\mathrm h})$, plus kinematic thresholds on $\eta$, $\pt$, and detector-volume spatial separation $(\Delta R = \sqrt{(\Delta \eta)^{2} + (\Delta \phi)^{2}})$. Lepton $\pt$ thresholds follow experimental trigger constraints from~\cite{CMS:2020wzx, CMS:2022goy, ATLAS:2021oiz}. For $\bq$-jet identification, we adopt~\cite{CMS_BTV2016} specifications: flat 70\% efficiency across the full $\pt$ range with 1\% misidentification rate. These parameters match the CMS DeepCSV algorithm's ``medium working point.'' Alternative configurations were examined: ``Loose'' (85\% efficiency, 10\% misidentification) and ``Tight'' (45\% efficiency, 0.1\% misidentification). Optimization studies established the ``medium working point'' as optimal for signal significance. 

DELPHES $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ identification emulates the deep neural network technique from~\cite{CMS_DeepTau}, where the DeepTau algorithm integrates isolation and $\tau$-lepton lifetime variables to discriminate $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ decay modes. Three DeepTau working points were evaluated per~\cite{CMS_DeepTau}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medium}: 70\% $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ efficiency, 0.5\% light-quark/gluon misidentification
    \item \textbf{Tight}: 60\% $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ efficiency, 0.2\% light-quark/gluon misidentification
    \item \textbf{VTight}: 50\% $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ efficiency, 0.1\% light-quark/gluon misidentification
\end{itemize}
Analogous to $\textrm{b}$-tagging optimization, $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$-tagging working point selection maximizes discovery sensitivity. The ``Medium'' configuration demonstrated superior performance and was adopted throughout. Muon (electron) identification assumes 95\% (85\%) efficiency with 0.3\% (0.6\%) misidentification~\cite{CMS-PAS-FTR-13-014,CMS_MUON_17001,CMS_EGM_17001}.

Following pre-selection, dominant background contributions arise from top quark pair production ($\tq\bar{ \tq}$) and single-top processes (single $\tq$), with subdominant contributions from vector boson plus jets ($V$+jets, from initial/final state radiation) and diboson production ($VV$). Two distinct signal sample sets support this analysis:

\textbf{Set 1 - Direct $\lq$ searches}: Explores $\set{M_{U},g_{U}}$ parameter space for $\beta_R\in \set{0,-1}$. Mass sampling spans 250 GeV to 5000 GeV (250 GeV steps), while coupling sampling ranges from 0.25 to 3.5 (0.25 steps). Although production cross-sections depend on both $M_{U}$ and $g_{U}$, selection efficiencies depend predominantly on $M_{U}$ owing to narrow-width conditions ($\frac{\Gamma_{U}}{M_{U}} < 5$\%), making experimental resolution the limiting factor. This set comprises 280 $\set{M_{U},g_{U},\beta_{R}}$ configurations, each with separate training and testing subsamples for machine learning development.

\textbf{Set 2 - Interference studies}: Quantifies $\lq$-$\zb^{\prime}$ interference in non-res production. Benchmark values $g_U=1.8$ and $\beta_R=0$ define the baseline, with $\set{M_{U},g_{\zb^{\prime}}}$ variations examined for two $\zb^{\prime}$ mass hierarchies: $\left(M_{\zb'}/M_U\right)^2 \in \Set{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}}$. Mass sampling covers 500 GeV to 5000 GeV (250 GeV steps), with $g_{\zb^{\prime}}$ ranging 0.25 to 3.5 (0.25 steps). This configuration yields 280 $\set{M_{U},g_{\zb^{\prime}},\left(M_{\zb'}/M_U\right)^2}$ scenarios, generating $6.0 \times 10^{5}$ MC events per scenario.


Simulated events undergo initial filtering based on experimental constraints: CMS detector geometry, standard particle reconstruction kinematic thresholds, and trigger availability. Events passing pre-selection train and test dedicated BDT classifiers for each $\set{M_{U},g_{U}}$ signal point, optimizing signal-background discrimination. BDT implementation leverages \texttt{scikit-learn}~\cite{pedregosa_scikit-learn_2011} and \texttt{xgboost} (XGB)~\cite{chen_xgboost_2016} Python libraries. The \texttt{XGBClassifier} class forms the core algorithm, with hyperparameter optimization via 10-fold cross-validation using \texttt{scikit-learn}'s \texttt{GridCV}\footnote{GridCV systematically identifies optimal hyperparameter combinations, critical for peak performance.} across a grid spanning: estimator counts [75, 125, 250, 500], maximum depths [3, 5, 7, 9], and learning rates [0.01, 0.1, 1, 10]. The cost function employs default mean squared error (\texttt{MSE}), while tree construction uses the histogram-optimized approximate greedy algorithm (\texttt{hist}) with uniform sampling. These selections maximize BDT detection capability through systematic hyperparameter tuning, appropriate loss functions, and efficient tree-building strategies.  


Binary XGB classifiers were developed for each of the six channels and each $\set{M_{U},g_{U}}$ signal point, employing a 20\% training / 80\% testing split applied uniformly across all signal and background MC samples.

Feature engineering examined over forty kinematic and topological observables, encompassing: b-jet and $\tau_{\textrm{h},\ell}$ candidate momenta; invariant and transverse masses of $\tau$ pairs and $\textrm{b}\,\tau$ systems; angular separations (b-b, $\tau$-$\tau$, and $\tau_{\textrm{h},\ell}$-b); plus missing-momentum-derived quantities. Correlation studies and performance assessments revealed that eight variables capture the majority of discriminating power.  
The most discriminating variable is the scalar transverse energy sum $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$, comprising final-state object $\pt$ ($\tau_{\mathrm h}$, $\tau_{h/\ell}$, $\bq$ jets) plus missing transverse momentum:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}=|\vec{p}_T^{\;miss}|+\sum_{\tau_{\mathrm h},\,\tau_{h/\ell},\,\bq}|\vec{p}_T|
\end{equation}
This observable, proven effective in LHC $\lq$ searches, directly probes resonant particle mass scales. Additional discriminating features include: vectorial $\pt$ difference magnitude between lepton candidates ($|\Delta \vec{p}_T |_{\tau_{\textrm{h}} \tau_{\textrm{h}/\ell}}$), their angular separation $\Delta R_{\tau_{\textrm{h}} \tau_{\textrm{h}/\ell}}$, reconstructed dilepton mass $m_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\textrm{h}/\ell}}$, and charge product ($Q_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}} \times Q_{\tau_{\textrm{h}/\ell}}$). The feature set incorporates $|\Delta \vec{p}_T|$ between $\tau_{\mathrm h}$ and $\vec{p}_T^{\,miss}$, plus (when present) $|\Delta \vec{p}_T|$ between $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ and the leading $\bq$ jet. For multi-$\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ final states, the highest-$\pt$ candidate defines these quantities. 

Fig.~\ref{fig:sT(TeV)_wRHC} presents key topological distributions for the $\bq\, \tau_{\mathrm h} \tau_{\ell}$ category, with $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$ shown in the top panel. All signal production mechanisms appear with relative weights reflecting their contribution to the combined signal, which is unity-normalized. Background processes appear individually, each unity-normalized. The combined signal exhibits s$\lq$ dominance at high $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$ values, transitioning to non-res dominance at low $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$. Backgrounds concentrate at low $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$ reflecting SM particle mass scales (top quarks, Z/W bosons), suggesting clear s$\lq$/d$\lq$ signal separability from SM processes. Consistent with resonant production, s$\lq$ and d$\lq$ $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$ distributions center near $M_U$ with broad widths—expected for large $M_{U}$ given that longitudinal momentum components are absent from $S_{\mathrm{T}}^{MET}$ calculations.

The second panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:sT(TeV)_wRHC} displays reconstructed ditau mass for the $\bq \tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}$ channel. Signal event $\tau$ candidates originate from distinct production vertices (e.g., separate $\lq$ decay chains in d$\lq$ production), yielding ditau mass scaling $m_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}} \sim \pt(\tau_{\textrm{h}}) + \pt(\tau_{\ell})$ with high-mass tails extending beyond SM predictions, dependent on $M_{U}$. Conversely, SM $m_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}}$ distributions concentrate near $m_{\textrm{Z/W}}$ since SM $\tau$ candidates arise from $\zb/\wb$ decays.

The third panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:sT(TeV)_wRHC} presents $\Delta R_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}}$ distributions for the $\textrm{b}\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}$ channel. For non-res ($\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}\to\tau\tau$) signal, momentum conservation mandates back-to-back $\tau$ leptons, producing $\Delta R_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}}$ distributions peaked near $\pi$ radians. In s$\lq$ production, while the $\lq$ and associated $\tau$ are back-to-back, the second $\tau$ (from $\lq$ decay alongside a b quark) need not align with the $\lq$ direction. Consequently, s$\lq$ $\Delta R_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}}$ distributions broaden with means below $\pi$ radians. For $\tq\bar{ \tq}$ background, $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ candidates frequently represent misidentified jets. Such fake $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ can originate from the same top decay chain as $\tau_{\ell}$, yielding small $\Delta R_{\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}}$ values. These distribution differences enable effective ML-based signal-background discrimination.

The $|\Delta \vec{p}_{T}|$ observable between visible $\tau$ candidates and b-quark jets provides crucial BDT discrimination. Fig.~\ref{fig:sT(TeV)_wRHC} (bottom panel) illustrates this with $|\Delta \vec{p}_{T}|$ between $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ and b-jet for the $\textrm{b}\tau_{\textrm{h}}\tau_{\ell}$ channel. In d$\lq$ production, $\lq \to \textrm{b}\tau$ decay imparts $p_{T} \sim M_{U}/2$ to b quarks and $\tau$ leptons. Hadronic tau decay ($\tau \to \tau_{\textrm{h}}\nu$) transfers substantial momentum to neutrinos, causing d$\lq$ (and s$\lq$) $|\Delta \vec{p}_{T}|_{\textrm{b}\tau_{\textrm{h}}}$ distributions to peak below $M_{U}$/2. For V+jets background, initial-state radiation produces b jets that momentum-balance the associated vector boson ($p_{T}(\textrm{b}) \sim p_{T}(\textrm{V}) \sim m_{\textrm{V}}$). Since visible $\tau$ candidates typically arise from V boson decay, their momentum averages $p_{T}(\tau_{\textrm{h}}) \sim p_{T}(\textrm{V})/4 \sim m_{\textrm{V}}/4$. First-order considerations thus predict V+jets $|\Delta \vec{p}_{T}|$ distributions peaking below $m_{\textrm{V}}$.

Fig.~\ref{fig:XGB_output} presents $\bq\tau_{\mathrm h}\tau_\ell$ BDT classifier results across signal production modes and backgrounds. Analogous to Fig.~\ref{fig:sT(TeV)_wRHC}, individual signal production mode distributions carry weights reflecting their combined signal contribution, with all distributions unity-normalized. The benchmark point ($M_{U} = 1.5$ TeV, $g_{U} = 1.8$, $\beta_{R} = -1$) defines these XGB distributions. XGB output spans [0,1], quantifying event signal-likeness (near 1) versus background-likeness (near 0). The s$\lq$ and d$\lq$ modes manifest as enhancements near unity, while backgrounds concentrate toward zero. Quantitatively, over 80\% of sLQ and dLQ distributions populate the final two bins (XGB $>$ 0.96), whereas more than 60\% of backgrounds occupy the first two bins (XGB $<$ 0.04).

Notably, the non-res distribution exhibits broader structure compared to sLQ/dLQ, with less pronounced peaking near XGB=1—consistent with the kinematic distinctions discussed previously. Examining the final bin specifically: approximately 0.2\% of background resides here, contrasted with 22\% non-res, 78\% sLQ, and 91\% dLQ. These fractions underscore XGB effectiveness for background suppression in signal-enriched regions.

XGB classifier output distributions (signal and background), normalized to cross-section $\times$ pre-selection efficiency $\times$ luminosity, undergo profile binned likelihood statistical testing to extract expected signal significance. The \texttt{RooFit}~\cite{RooFit} package implements this estimation following methodologies from~\cite{Barbosa:2022mmw, Florez:2021zoo, Florez:2019tqr, Florez:2018ojp, Florez:2017xhf, VBFZprimePaper, Florez:2016lwi, U1T3R, mSUGRApaper, SupercriticalString, ConnectingPPandCosmology, VBF1, DMmodels2, VBFSlepton, VBFStop, VBFSbottom}. Significance ($Z_{sig}$) measurement leverages the probability of obtaining identical test statistic outcomes under background-only versus signal-plus-background hypotheses. This procedure yields local p-values, enabling significance calculation where the Gaussian tail integral from $Z_{sig}$ to $\infty$ equals the local p-value. 

Systematic uncertainties enter as nuisance parameters: log-priors for normalization systematics, Gaussian priors for shape systematics. Our treatment encompasses dominant experimental and theoretical uncertainty sources:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Luminosity}: 3\% uncertainty on integrated luminosity measurement~\cite{lumiRef}
    \item \textbf{PDF choice}: 5\% uncertainty from PDF4LHC prescription~\cite{Butterworth:2015oua}, affecting yields while leaving XGB output shapes negligibly impacted
    \item \textbf{$\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ identification}: Conservative 5\% per $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ candidate (independent of $p_{\textrm{T}}$, $\eta$), per~\cite{CMS_DeepTau}'s reported 2-5\% range. Correlated between signal/background processes with genuine $\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ and across XGB bins within each process
    \item \textbf{$\tau_{\textrm{h}}$ energy scale}: 5\% uncertainty (independent of $p_{\textrm{T}}$, $\eta$), following CMS measurements~\cite{CMS_DeepTau}
    \item \textbf{b-tagging}: Conservative 3\% per b-jet~\cite{CMSbtag}
    \item \textbf{Background modeling}: Additional 10\% on all background predictions to accommodate potential simulation mismodeling
\end{itemize}
Background uncertainty estimates typically derive from collision data via dedicated control samples with negligible signal contamination, enriched in specific backgrounds. Background systematic uncertainties are treated as uncorrelated between processes.
