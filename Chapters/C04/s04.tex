\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:discusion}

The exploration of leptoquarks with enhanced couplings to the heaviest fermion generation remains motivated by persistent tensions in semileptonic $\Bm$-meson decay measurements, particularly the ratios $R_D$ and $R_{D^*}$ relative to SM predictions. While experimental collaborations have conducted numerous $\lq$ searches, a comprehensive assessment across different theoretical assumptions within specific model frameworks is essential for identifying optimal discovery strategies. Moreover, advanced computational methods based on machine learning offer promising avenues to enhance signal discrimination capabilities beyond traditional cut-based approaches. This chapter presents a detailed phenomenological investigation of $U_1$ leptoquark production and decay, examining various coupling structures, mass hypotheses, and chiral current configurations. We analyze proton-proton collisions at center-of-mass energies of $\sqrt{s} = 13\tev$ and $13.6\tev$ across multiple integrated luminosity scenarios, extending projections to the high-luminosity LHC era. The analysis employs gradient-boosted decision tree classifiers to optimize signal-background separation, with the classifier output serving as input to a profile binned-likelihood statistical framework for extracting discovery significance. 

Our analysis presents discovery reach and exclusion sensitivity in the two-dimensional parameter space of the leptoquark coupling $g_U$ versus its mass $M_U$, examining single-$\lq$ (s$\lq$), double-$\lq$ (d$\lq$), and non-resonant (non-res) production modes both individually and in combination. Results are shown for three coupling scenarios: exclusive left-handed interactions, mixed chirality, and exclusive right-handed interactions. For scenarios compatible with addressing the $\Bm$-meson anomalies, we overlay the theoretically favored parameter regions. Consistent with earlier phenomenological studies, we confirm that optimal sensitivity arises from combining d$\lq$ production (dominant at weak couplings due to QCD processes) with non-res processes (dominant at strong couplings due to quartic coupling dependence). The discovery potential exhibits strong chirality dependence, with both the accessible parameter space and the anomaly-preferred region shifting according to the chosen coupling structure. Across all evaluated configurations, current LHC data begins to probe the upper mass range of the B-physics anomaly solution space.

Comparison of our projected exclusion contours with published ATLAS and CMS analyses indicates enhanced sensitivity from the machine learning-based approach relative to standard cut-based methods, particularly for large coupling values. Furthermore, HL-LHC projections demonstrate comprehensive coverage of the parameter space favored by $\Bm$-physics anomalies, extending to leptoquark masses approaching $5\tev$. These projections account for systematic uncertainties in luminosity determination, parton distribution functions, $\tau_{\mathrm{h}}$ identification and energy scale, $\bq$-jet tagging efficiency, and background normalization, providing realistic estimates of future discovery potential.

We investigate the role of an associated $\zb^{\prime}$ gauge boson in modifying non-resonant production kinematics and rates, as naturally arises in gauge-complete frameworks such as Pati-Salam constructions. The $\zb^{\prime}$ contribution introduces significant interference effects that can either enhance or suppress the discovery reach depending on the relative masses and coupling strengths of the two mediators. For $M_{\zb^{\prime}} < M_U$, constructive interference generally increases sensitivity, while $M_{\zb^{\prime}} > M_U$ scenarios exhibit complex destructive interference patterns at large leptoquark masses. Given that non-resonant channels provide leading sensitivity across substantial regions of parameter space, particularly at large couplings, understanding these interference patterns represents a crucial aspect of comprehensive leptoquark searches at hadron colliders.
