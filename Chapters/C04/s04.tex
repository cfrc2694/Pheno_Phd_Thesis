\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:discusion}

The exploration of leptoquarks with enhanced couplings to the heaviest fermion generation remains motivated by persistent tensions in semileptonic $\Bm$-meson decay measurements, particularly the ratios $R_D$ and $R_{D^*}$ relative to SM predictions. While experimental collaborations have conducted numerous $\lq$ searches, a comprehensive assessment across different theoretical assumptions within specific model frameworks is essential for identifying optimal discovery strategies. Moreover, advanced computational methods based on machine learning offer promising avenues to enhance signal discrimination capabilities beyond traditional cut-based approaches. This chapter presents a detailed phenomenological investigation of $U_1$ leptoquark production and decay, examining various coupling structures, mass hypotheses, and chiral current configurations. We analyze proton-proton collisions at center-of-mass energies of $\sqrt{s} = 13\tev$ and $13.6\tev$ across multiple integrated luminosity scenarios, extending projections to the high-luminosity LHC era. The analysis employs gradient-boosted decision tree classifiers to optimize signal-background separation, with the classifier output serving as input to a profile binned-likelihood statistical framework for extracting discovery significance. 

Our analysis presents discovery reach and exclusion sensitivity in the two-dimensional parameter space of the leptoquark coupling $g_U$ versus its mass $M_U$, examining single-$\lq$ (s$\lq$), double-$\lq$ (d$\lq$), and non-resonant (non-res) production modes both individually and in combination. Results are shown for three coupling scenarios: exclusive left-handed interactions, mixed chirality, and exclusive right-handed interactions. For scenarios compatible with addressing the $\Bm$-meson anomalies, we overlay the theoretically favored parameter regions. Consistent with earlier phenomenological studies, we confirm that optimal sensitivity arises from combining d$\lq$ production (dominant at weak couplings due to QCD processes) with non-res processes (dominant at strong couplings due to quartic coupling dependence). The discovery potential exhibits strong chirality dependence, with both the accessible parameter space and the anomaly-preferred region shifting according to the chosen coupling structure. Across all evaluated configurations, current LHC data begins to probe the upper mass range of the B-physics anomaly solution space.

Comparison of our projected exclusion contours with published ATLAS and CMS analyses indicates enhanced sensitivity from the machine learning-based approach relative to standard cut-based methods, particularly for large coupling values. Furthermore, HL-LHC projections demonstrate comprehensive coverage of the parameter space favored by $\Bm$-physics anomalies, extending to leptoquark masses approaching $5\tev$. These projections account for systematic uncertainties in luminosity determination, parton distribution functions, $\tau_{\mathrm{h}}$ identification and energy scale, $\bq$-jet tagging efficiency, and background normalization, providing realistic estimates of future discovery potential.

We investigate the role of an associated $\zb^{\prime}$ gauge boson in modifying non-resonant production kinematics and rates, as naturally arises in gauge-complete frameworks such as Pati-Salam constructions. The $\zb^{\prime}$ contribution introduces significant interference effects that can either enhance or suppress the discovery reach depending on the relative masses and coupling strengths of the two mediators. For $M_{\zb^{\prime}} < M_U$, constructive interference generally increases sensitivity, while $M_{\zb^{\prime}} > M_U$ scenarios exhibit complex destructive interference patterns at large leptoquark masses. Given that non-resonant channels provide leading sensitivity across substantial regions of parameter space, particularly at large couplings, understanding these interference patterns represents a crucial aspect of comprehensive leptoquark searches at hadron colliders.

While this study focuses on benchmark scenarios exploring different chirality structures through their impact on branching ratios and production cross sections, a complete discrimination between left-handed, right-handed, and mixed coupling scenarios requires additional observables beyond total rates. The complete phenomenology of the $U_1$ model must account for constraints from precision flavor measurements, which have been systematically explored in the context of 4321 models that provide the underlying structure for the coupling matrix. Complementary bounds arise from rare $\Bm$-decays and lepton flavor violation processes, constraining the parameter space in regions where direct collider searches may have limited reach~\cite{Belle-II:2025bho,LHCb:2021trn,Belle-II:2018jsg}.

An important direction for future experimental and phenomenological work involves detailed studies of $\tau$ polarization in ditau final states from leptoquark production and decay. The polarization of $\tau$ leptons provides a powerful discriminant between different chiral structures, as left-handed and right-handed leptoquark couplings produce characteristically different polarization patterns in the final state. Measuring $\tau$ polarization through the angular distributions of decay products—particularly in single-prong hadronic decays $\tau \to \pi \nu$ and $\tau \to \rho \nu$, as well as leptonic channels—can provide model-independent information about the Lorentz structure of the leptoquark interactions. Such measurements would complement the rate-based analyses presented here and could definitively establish the chirality of leptoquark couplings in the event of a discovery. These polarization studies represent an essential component of the comprehensive leptoquark program at the LHC, with particular relevance for distinguishing between $\beta_R = 0$ (pure left-handed), $\beta_R = -1$ (mixed), and pure right-handed coupling scenarios. Future phenomenological work should develop optimized observables and analysis strategies for extracting polarization information from reconstructed ditau events, accounting for the challenges posed by neutrinos in the final state and the dilution of polarization information through the $\tau$ decay process.

Beyond leptoquark searches, high-$\pt$ measurements in the ditau channel provide independent sensitivity to the coupling $g_U$ through non-resonant $t$-channel exchange. As demonstrated in various recast analyses~\cite{Faroughy:2016osc,Schmaltz:2018nls,Baker:2019sli,GINO_2022}, enhancements in the high-invariant-mass and high-transverse-momentum tails of the $\tau\tau$ spectrum probe leptoquarks with masses beyond the kinematic reach for on-shell production. These tail measurements complement resonant searches and remain sensitive to virtual leptoquark contributions in the contact interaction regime, extending discovery potential to multi-TeV mass scales for couplings $g_U \gtrsim 1$. The combination of resonant production, non-resonant tail measurements, and polarization observables provides a comprehensive strategy for exploring the parameter space of third-generation leptoquark models.

Looking forward, the HL-LHC program with $3000~\fb^{-1}$ of integrated luminosity will extend both mass reach and coupling sensitivity. Advances in detector technology and reconstruction algorithms—particularly for $\tau$ identification and $\bq$-tagging in high-pileup environments—will further enhance discrimination power. The machine learning techniques developed in this study represent an important proof of principle, but future analyses may benefit from more sophisticated architectures such as graph neural networks or attention mechanisms that can better exploit the full event topology and incorporate polarization-sensitive observables. Beyond the LHC, proposed future colliders offer complementary discovery potential: high-energy muon colliders could directly produce leptoquark pairs through $\mu^+ \mu^- \to U_1 \bar{U}_1$, while electron-proton colliders would provide clean environments for studying single-leptoquark production.
