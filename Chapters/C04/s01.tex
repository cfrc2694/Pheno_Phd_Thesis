\section{A Simplified Model for the $U_1$ Leptoquark}
\label{sec:model}

Incorporating a massive $U_1$ vector $\lq$ into the SM presents theoretical challenges, primarily concerning model renormalizability. Theoretical efforts have concentrated on Pati-Salam (PS) framework extensions that circumvent proton decay, exemplified by~\cite{Assad:2017iib}. Additional realizations include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item PS extensions featuring vector-like fermions~\cite{Calibbi:2017qbu,Blanke:2018sro,Iguro:2021kdw}
    \item The 4321 models~\cite{DiLuzio:2017vat,Greljo:2018tuh,DiLuzio:2018zxy}
    \item The twin PS$^2$ framework~\cite{King:2021jeo,FernandezNavarro:2022gst}
    \item The three-site PS$^3$ construction~\cite{Bordone:2017bld,Bordone:2018nbg,Fuentes-Martin:2022xnb}
    \item Composite PS scenarios~\cite{Gripaios:2009dq,Barbieri:2016las,Barbieri:2017tuq}
\end{itemize}

Our analysis employs a simplified non-renormalizable Lagrangian, understood as an effective description embedded within a complete ultraviolet theory. SM extension proceeds through the following $U_1$ $\lq$ interaction terms:
\begin{eqnarray}
\label{eq:BasicLagrangian}
  \mathcal{L}_{U_1}&=&-\frac{1}{2}U^\dagger_{\mu\nu}U^{\mu\nu}+M_U^2\, U_{1\mu}^\dagger U_1^\mu \nonumber \\
 &&  -ig_s\,U_{1\mu}^\dagger\, T^a\, U_{1\nu}\, G^{a\mu\nu}\!\!-i\frac{2}{3}g'\,U^\dagger_{1\mu}U_{1\nu}B^{\mu\nu} \nonumber \\
 && +\frac{g_U}{\sqrt 2}[U_{1\mu}(\bar Q_3\,\gamma^\mu L_3+\beta_L^{s\tau}\,\bar Q_2\,\gamma^\mu L_3 \nonumber \\  && +\beta_{R}\,\bar b_{R}\,\gamma^\mu \tau_{R}) +{\rm h.c.}] 
\end{eqnarray}
with $U_{\mu\nu}\equiv\mathcal{D}_\mu U_{1\nu}-\mathcal{D}_\nu U_{1\mu}$ and $\mathcal{D}_\mu\equiv\partial_\mu+ig_s T^a G_\mu^a+i\tfrac{2}{3}g'B_\mu$. The second line reflects our assumption of gauge origin for the $\lq$ \footnote{Literature presentations of Eq.~(\ref{eq:BasicLagrangian})'s second-line couplings employ $g_s\to g_s(1-\kappa_U)$ and $g'\to g'(1-\tilde\kappa_U)$ parameterizations, accommodating potential underlying strong dynamics.}.

Eq.~(\ref{eq:BasicLagrangian})'s third and fourth lines describe $\lq$-SM fermion interactions through coupling $g_U$, configured to favor third-generation fermions~\footnote{Prior to resolution of the $R_{K^{(*)}}$ anomaly~\cite{LHCb:2022qnv,LHCb:2022zom,Greljo:2022jac,Ciuchini:2022wbq}, analyses employed a complete $3\times3$ $\beta_L$ matrix fitted to address all $\Bm$ meson anomalies.}. These couplings critically govern both $\lq$ decay branching fractions and single-$\lq$ production rates. We adopt $\beta_L^{s\tau} = 0.2$ (the $\lq \to s\tau$ coupling strength) following~\cite{Cornella:2021sby}, a choice that simultaneously accommodates the $R_{D^{(*)}}$ anomaly while satisfying $\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}\to\tau^+\tau^-$ experimental bounds. While $\beta_L^{s\tau}$ formally modifies single-$\lq$ production cross sections and decay widths, verification studies confirm that $\beta_L^{s\tau} = 0.2$ produces negligible collider phenomenology impact, justifying its omission from subsequent analyses.

The parameter $\beta_R$ controls right-handed coupling strength relative to left-handed interactions, carrying significant phenomenological consequences for both $\lq$ branching fractions \footnote{Non-zero $\beta_L^{s\tau}$ activates additional decay channels, though $\beta_L^{s\tau}$ and CKM suppression factors ($\lambda_{\rm CKM}$) limit their impact to $<3\%$ modifications of ${\rm BR}(\lq \to \bq\,\tau)$ and ${\rm BR}(\lq \to \tq\,\nu)$.} and single-$\lq$ production rates. Fig.~\ref{fig:branching_ratios} (left) illustrates $\lq\to\textrm{b}\tau$ and $\lq\to\textrm{t}\nu$ branching fractions versus $\lq$ mass for two $\beta_R$ scenarios. Key observations include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For $\beta_R = 0$ at large masses: ${\rm BR}(\lq \to \bq\,\tau) \approx {\rm BR}(\lq \to \tq\,\nu)\approx \tfrac{1}{2}$
    \item For $\beta_R = -1$ (as in~\cite{Cornella:2019hct}): additional amplitude contributions yield ${\rm BR}(\lq\to \bq\,\tau) \approx \tfrac{2}{3}$
\end{itemize}
This branching ratio enhancement diminishes constraints from $\tq\,\nu$-focused searches, motivating dedicated $\mathrm{b}\tau$ final state investigations. While exclusive right-handed coupling ($g_U\to0$ with $g_U\beta_R\not=0$) could achieve ${\rm BR}(\lq\to \bq\,\tau) \approx 1$, such configurations fail to explain observed $R_{D^{(*)}}$ anomalies. Consequently, despite some LHC searches assuming ${\rm BR}(\lq\to \bq\,\tau) = 1$, our analysis employs parameter values consistent with $R_{D^{(*)}}$ anomaly resolution.

Fig.~\ref{fig:branching_ratios} (right) elucidates $\beta_R$ collider phenomenology through production cross sections for single-$\lq$ (s$\lq$), pair (d$\lq$), and non-resonant (non-res) channels versus mass, assuming $g_{U} = 1.8$ and $\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}$ collisions at $\sqrt{s} = 13$ $\tev$. This benchmark yields narrow-width conditions: $\Gamma(\lq\to\textrm{b}\tau) <5\%$ of $m_{\lq}$ across $250$ $\gev$ $-$ $2.5$ $\tev$. Key production mechanism dependencies emerge:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pair production (d$\lq$)}: QCD-dominated processes render cross sections insensitive to $\beta_R$
    \item \textbf{Single production (s$\lq$)}: Non-zero $\beta_R$ enhances rates by factor $\sim2$
    \item \textbf{Non-resonant}: $\beta_R\neq0$ boosts cross sections by nearly one order of magnitude
\end{itemize}
These $\beta_R$ dependencies follow naturally from production diagrams in Fig.~\ref{fig:feynmp-prod-channels}. The mass threshold where s$\lq$ exceeds d$\lq$ production depends on $g_U$ magnitude. 
 
Addressing the $R_{D^{(*)}}$ anomaly imposes constraints on the Wilson coefficient $C_U\equiv g^2_U\,v^2_{SM}/(4\,M^2_U)$, with allowed ranges depending on $\beta_{R}$ as demonstrated in~\cite{Cornella:2021sby}. Consequently, viable $g_{U}$ values correlate with both $M_{U}$ and $\beta_{R}$, necessitating exploration across this multi-dimensional parameter space.


Our investigation incorporates $\zb'$ boson effects on $\mathrm{pp}\to\tau\tau$ production, motivated by theoretical constructions in~\cite{Baker:2019sli} and the 4321 framework~\cite{DiLuzio:2017vat,GINO_PhysRevD.102.115015}. Minimal SM extensions hosting massive gauge $U_1$ leptoquarks utilize gauge group $SU(4)\times SU(3)^{\prime}\times SU(2)_L \times U(1)_{T_R^3}$, whose spontaneous breaking to SM structure inevitably produces additional massive vectors: a $\zb^{\prime}$ boson and color-octet $G'$ (details in App.~\ref{sec:4321}). \marginpar{Breaking origins: leptoquarks from $SU(4)\to SU(3)_{[4]}\times U(1)_{B-L}$, coloron $G'$ from $SU(3)_{[4]}\times SU(3)'\to SU(3)_c$, and $Z'$ from $U(1)_{B-L}\times U(1)_{T_R^3}\to U(1)_Y$. Mass and coupling relations emerge from scalar potential structure.} The $\zb'$ substantially influences $\lq$ discovery through s-channel $\mathrm{pp}\to\tau\tau$ contributions (resonant and virtual). We augment Eq.~(\ref{eq:BasicLagrangian}) with $\zb'$ interactions:
\begin{eqnarray}
    \label{eq:BasicLagrangianZp}
        \mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}}&= & -\frac{1}{4} Z_{\mu \nu}^{\prime} Z^{\prime \mu \nu}+\frac{1}{2} M_{Z^{\prime}}^2 Z_\mu^{\prime} Z^{\prime \mu} \nonumber \\
        && + \frac{g_{Z^{\prime}}}{2 \sqrt{6}} Z^{\prime \mu} (\zeta_q \bar{Q}_3 \gamma_\mu Q_3 +\zeta_t \bar{t}_R \gamma_\mu t_R \nonumber \\
        &&  +\zeta_b \bar{b}_R \gamma_\mu b_R-3 \zeta_{\ell} \bar{L}_3 \gamma_\mu L_3-3 \zeta_\tau \bar{\tau}_R \gamma_\mu \tau_R),
\end{eqnarray}
where $M_{\zb^{\prime}}$ denotes the $\zb'$ mass, $g_{Z^{\prime}}$ its gauge coupling, and $\zeta_q$, $\zeta_t$, $\zeta_b$, $\zeta_{\ell}$, $\zeta_\tau$ represent generation-dependent flavor couplings.

Following~\cite{GINO_PhysRevD.102.115015}, to connect this phenomenological Lagrangian to the underlying 4321 theory, we review the gauge coupling relations. The fundamental couplings ($g_4$, $g_3$, $g_1$) map to SM observables ($g_s$, $g_Y$) via~\cite{DiLuzio2018}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coupling_matching}
g_s = \frac{g_4 g_3}{\sqrt{g_4^2 + g_3^2}}, \qquad
g_Y = \frac{g_4 g_1}{\sqrt{g_4^2 + \tfrac{2}{3} g_1^2}}.
\end{equation}
At $\mu=2$ TeV, RGE evolution yields $g_s = 1.02$ and $g_Y = 0.363$. Since matching relations enforce $g_s \leq g_{4,3}$ and $g_Y \leq \sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}} g_{4}$, $g_{1}$, the observed hierarchy $g_s \gg g_Y$ propagates to fundamental couplings: $g_{4,3} \gg g_Y \simeq g_1$. The massive gauge boson spectrum reflects both gauge coupling and VEV hierarchies. Assuming scalar VEV ordering $v_3 \gg v_1 \gg v_{15}$ alongside gauge coupling parity $g_4 \sim g_3$, the mass expressions (App.~\ref{sec:4321})
\begin{align}
M^2_{U} &= \frac{1}{4} g_4^2 \left(v_1^2 + v_3^2 + \tfrac{4}{3} v_{15}^2\right),\label{eq:mass_expressions_a}\\
M^2_{g'} &= \frac{1}{2}  (g_4^2 + g_3^2) v_3^2, \label{eq:mass_expressions_b}\\
M^2_{Z'} &= \frac{1}{8} \left( g_4^2 + \tfrac{2}{3} g_1^2 \right) \left(3v_1^2 + v_3^2 \right)    \label{eq:mass_expressions_c}
\end{align}
simplify under the stated hierarchies: neglecting $v_{15}$ and using $g_{4,3} \gg g_1$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
M_U^2 &\simeq \frac{1}{4} g_4^2 (v_1^2 + v_3^2), &
M_{Z'}^2 &\simeq \frac{1}{8} g_4^2 (3v_1^2 + v_3^2).
\end{align*}
The ratio $2(M_{Z'}/M_U)^2$ then becomes
\[
2\left(\frac{M_{Z'}}{M_U}\right)^2 \simeq \frac{3v_1^2 + v_3^2}{v_1^2 + v_3^2} = \frac{3r+1}{r+1},
\]
where $r \equiv v_1^2/v_3^2 > 0$. This function increases monotonically with $r$, ranging from $1$ (when $r\to 0$, i.e. $v_3 \gg v_1$) to $3$ (when $r\to \infty$, i.e. $v_1 \gg v_3$). Hence, we obtain the inequality chain
\[
1 \;<\; 2\left(\frac{M_{Z'}}{M_U}\right)^2 \;<\; 3,
\]
which brackets the theoretically allowed range of $M_{Z'}$ relative to $M_U$. The naive limit $v_3 \gg v_1$ yields $M_{g'} \simeq \sqrt{2} M_U$ and $M_{Z'} \simeq M_U/\sqrt{2}$ (corresponding to the lower bound), while the opposite hierarchy $v_1 \gg v_3$ gives $M_{Z'} \simeq \sqrt{3/2}\,M_U$ (the upper bound). Motivated by this range—though permitting model-dependent deviations—we examine two $\zb'$ mass hierarchy benchmarks: light and heavy scenarios that bracket the theoretically allowed region. Throughout, we assume exclusive left-handed couplings ($\zeta_q=\zeta_\ell= 1$, $\zeta_t=\zeta_b=\zeta_\tau=0$) consistent with 4321 chiral structure. Fig.~\ref{fig:interference} quantifies interference effects through the relative kinematic interference parameter ($\mathrm{RKI}$) versus reconstructed $m_{\tau\tau}$ for $g_{\zb^{\prime}} = 1$ across multiple $M_U$ values, defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:RKI}
    \mathrm{RKI}(m_{\tau\tau})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\lq+\zb'}}\left[\frac{d\sigma_{\lq+\zb'}}{dm_{\tau\tau}}-\left(\frac{d\sigma_{\lq}}{dm_{\tau\tau}}+\frac{d\sigma_{\zb'}}{dm_{\tau\tau}}\right)\right],
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{X}$ denotes production cross section from particle set $X$. The inclusive cross section $\sigma_{\lq+\zb'}$ incorporates both virtual $\lq$ and s-channel $\zb'$ amplitudes. 

The interference structure emerges from distinct Mandelstam-variable dependencies: non-resonant leptoquark production proceeds via t-channel exchange with amplitude $\mathcal{M}_{U_1} \sim (t-m_{U_1}^2 + i m_{U_1} \Gamma_{U_1})^{-1}$, while resonant $\zb'$ production utilizes s-channel propagation with amplitude $\mathcal{M}_{Z'} \sim (s-m_{Z'}^2 + i m_{Z'} \Gamma_{Z'})^{-1}$. The interference contribution to the differential cross section scales as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interference_formula}
\frac{d\sigma_{\rm int}}{dm_{\tau\tau}} \sim \frac{g_{Z'}g_{U}}{s}\frac{ m_{U}m_{Z'}\Gamma_{U}\Gamma_{Z'} - (t-m_{U}^2)(s-m_{Z'}^2)}{\left[(t-m_{U}^2)^2 + m_{U}^2\Gamma_{U}^2\right]\left[(s-m_{Z'}^2)^2 + m_{Z'}^2\Gamma_{Z'}^2\right]}.
\end{equation}
In the narrow-width approximation, the term $m_{U}m_{Z'}\Gamma_{U}\Gamma_{Z'}$ is negligible. Since $(t-m_{U}^2) < 0$ for physical kinematics, the sign of the interference is governed by $- (t-m_{U}^2)(s-m_{Z'}^2)$. Below the $\zb'$ resonance ($s < m_{Z'}^2$), $(s-m_{Z'}^2) < 0$, yielding a positive product $(t-m_{U}^2)(s-m_{Z'}^2) > 0$ and thus destructive interference (negative numerator). Above the resonance ($s > m_{Z'}^2$), $(s-m_{Z'}^2) > 0$ leads to $(t-m_{U}^2)(s-m_{Z'}^2) < 0$, producing constructive interference (positive numerator). The $1/s$ prefactor suppresses interference at large $m_{\tau\tau}$, while near $m_{\tau\tau} \to 0$ the denominator enhances destructive effects. Consequently, both mass hierarchies exhibit pronounced RKI minima at low $m_{\tau\tau}$, signaling destructive $\lq$-$\zb'$ interference that suppresses differential cross sections and reduces integrated rates. These interference patterns align with comprehensive resonant and non-resonant $\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{p}\to\tq \bar{\tq}$ production studies in~\cite{Djouadi:2019cbm}.
