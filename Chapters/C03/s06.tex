\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Over the coming decade, the LHC program will accumulate pp collision data at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$~\textrm{TeV}, with the high-luminosity upgrade substantially increasing the total integrated luminosity. This enhanced dataset motivates investigation of previously unexplored regions of BSM parameter space, particularly those involving non-standard signatures that may have eluded detection in conventional search channels. The use of sophisticated analysis methods, especially machine learning techniques, becomes essential for extracting signals from backgrounds in kinematic regimes characterized by modest production rates and challenging discriminating features. 

The theoretical framework explored in this chapter involves a $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ gauge extension of the SM, offering potential explanations for several outstanding puzzles including the fermion mass hierarchy, the cosmological dark matter density, and tensions in precision measurements such as the muon $g - 2$ and the $R_{(D)}$ and $R_{(D^*)}$ observables. The model's particle content includes a light scalar $\phi'$ with mass potentially below the electroweak scale, along with TeV-mass vector-like quarks $\chi_\mathrm{u}$. Following Ref.~\cite{Dutta2020}, we focus on the case where $\phi'$ exhibits generation-dependent couplings to fermions with $m(\phi') \ge 1$~\textrm{GeV}, leading to dominant decays into muon pairs. While earlier studies~\cite{Dutta2023, Banerjee_2016} explored signatures involving merged diphoton systems from $\phi' \to \gamma\gamma$, the topology investigated here provides crucial sensitivity in the mass window $m(\phi') < 2 m_{\mathrm{t}}$ where top-antitop final states are kinematically inaccessible. 

The central finding of this chapter demonstrates that light new bosons spanning the GeV to electroweak mass range can be effectively probed at the LHC through an unconventional approach: exploiting the associated production of heavy colored states whose decays yield energetic SM particles within the acceptance of the ATLAS and CMS central detectors. By leveraging boosted kinematics and employing state-of-the-art machine learning classifiers, this strategy enables signal discrimination against softer SM processes. This search methodology offers discovery potential for promptly-decaying light particles, with the key insight that sensitivity to low-mass states improves significantly when the heavy UV degrees of freedom can be directly produced on-shell. The generational structure of the scalar couplings, particularly the connection to third-generation quarks, exemplifies a signature that would remain largely inaccessible to fixed-target and beam-dump experiments~\cite{Cacciapaglia_2023}.

This analysis approach provides an effective complement to existing LHC search programs, with projected HL-LHC sensitivity covering $\phi'$ masses between 1 and 325~\textrm{GeV} and vector-like quark masses $m(\chi_{\mathrm{u}})$ reaching approximately 2~\textrm{TeV}. Given these competitive projections, implementation of this search strategy by the ATLAS and CMS experiments would significantly extend the discovery reach for this class of BSM scenarios.

While this study focuses on benchmark scenarios that illustrate the discovery potential of the proposed search strategy, a complete phenomenological analysis of the $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model requires systematic exploration of the full parameter space and careful consideration of all experimental constraints. The model contains a rich set of parameters beyond the masses $m(\phi')$, $m(\chi_\mathrm{u})$, and $m(Z')$, including the $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ gauge coupling, the vacuum expectation value of the symmetry-breaking scalar field, the Yukawa couplings of the vector-like fermions, and the mixing angles between SM fermions and their vector-like partners. The phenomenology is highly sensitive to these parameters, which govern not only the collider signatures studied here but also precision observables, rare processes, and cosmological constraints. Future theoretical work should perform comprehensive parameter scans, incorporating Markov Chain Monte Carlo or nested sampling techniques to map the viable parameter space while simultaneously satisfying constraints from multiple experimental fronts (see e.g. Ref.~\cite{Ardila-Tafurth:2025qdf}).

Beyond its direct collider signatures, the complete viability of the $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model must be assessed against a broad spectrum of cosmological and low-energy constraints. The $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model naturally accommodates dark matter candidates through the right-handed neutrino sector required for anomaly cancellation. If the lightest right-handed neutrino is stable or sufficiently long-lived, it can constitute the observed dark matter relic density. This connection imposes stringent constraints on the model parameters. The relic abundance, determined by the freeze-out mechanism, depends sensitively on the $Z'$ and $\phi'$ masses and their couplings to neutrinos, with current cosmological measurements requiring $\Omega_{\mathrm{DM}} h^2 = 0.120 \pm 0.001$~\cite{Planck:2018vyg}. Direct detection experiments constrain the scattering cross sections of dark matter with nucleons, thereby limiting the $Z'$ and $\phi'$ couplings to quarks~\cite{XENON:2026qow,LUX-ZEPLIN:2022qhg}, while indirect detection searches for annihilation products in cosmic rays and gamma rays provide complementary bounds on the annihilation cross section~\cite{Bertone2005_DM_review}. A comprehensive study must ensure consistency between the collider signatures, the cosmological abundance, and the null results from direct and indirect detection experiments, which typically requires careful choices of the neutrino mass spectrum and mixing patterns.

The family non-universal couplings of $\phi'$ and the mixing between SM quarks and vector-like quarks induce flavor-changing neutral currents (FCNCs) that contribute to rare processes constrained by precision flavor experiments. Particularly stringent bounds arise from meson-antimeson oscillations ($K^0$-$\bar{K}^0$, $B^0_d$-$\bar{B}^0_d$, $B^0_s$-$\bar{B}^0_s$, $D^0$-$\bar{D}^0$), which constrain off-diagonal elements in the quark mass matrix and mixing angles with vector-like quarks. Rare decays such as $B \to K^{(*)} \mu^+ \mu^-$, $B_s \to \mu^+ \mu^-$, $B \to X_s \nu\bar{\nu}$~\cite{Belle-II:2025bho}, and $K \to \pi \nu \bar{\nu}$ are sensitive to new scalar and vector boson contributions at the loop level~\cite{Dutta2022}. While the preferential coupling to third-generation fermions partially evades constraints from kaon physics, the model must still satisfy bounds from $b \to s \ell^+ \ell^-$ transitions measured by LHCb, ATLAS, and CMS~\cite{LHCb:2021trn}. In the lepton sector, the $Z'$ and $\phi'$ contributions to lepton flavor violating processes such as $\mu \to e \gamma$, $\mu \to e$ conversion in nuclei, and $\tau \to \mu \gamma$ must remain below current experimental limits from MEG II~\cite{MEGII:2025gzr}, Mu2e~\cite{Ganguly:2022ufq}, and Belle II~\cite{Belle-II:2018jsg}. These flavor constraints often provide the most stringent bounds on certain combinations of couplings and masses, particularly in regions where collider searches have limited sensitivity. A thorough theoretical treatment requires computing these contributions at the one-loop level and ensuring consistency with the measured values of CKM matrix elements and CP-violating observables.


The right-handed neutrinos $\nu_R$ in the $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model play a crucial role in generating SM neutrino masses through mixing with the vector-like fermions and the Higgs mechanism. The observed neutrino oscillation patterns, characterized by the PMNS mixing matrix and the mass-squared differences $\Delta m^2_{21} \approx 7.5 \times 10^{-5}~\mathrm{eV}^2$ and $\Delta m^2_{31} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-3}~\mathrm{eV}^2$~\cite{Capozzi:2025ovi}, impose constraints on the Yukawa couplings and mixing angles in the neutrino sector. The model must reproduce the correct hierarchy (normal or inverted) and mixing patterns while remaining consistent with bounds on the sum of neutrino masses from cosmology, $\sum m_\nu < 0.12$~eV (95\% CL)~\cite{Planck:2018vyg}. If lepton number violation is present, the model contributes to neutrinoless double-beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$), with experimental bounds from GERDA~\cite{GERDA:2020xhi}, KamLAND-Zen~\cite{KamLAND-Zen:2024eml}, and future projects such as nEXO~\cite{nEXO:2021ujk} constraining the effective Majorana mass $|m_{\beta\beta}| \lesssim 0.1$~eV. The $Z'$ and $\phi'$ bosons can also contribute to these processes through virtual exchanges, modifying the standard interpretation of $0\nu\beta\beta$ results. Furthermore, the model's predictions for leptonic universality violation in charged-current interactions must be reconciled with precision measurements of $W$ boson decays and $\tau$ decays. A complete theoretical analysis requires constructing viable neutrino mass matrices, ensuring stability under renormalization group evolution, and demonstrating consistency across the full range of neutrino observables.

Beyond collider searches, low-energy precision experiments provide orthogonal probes of the $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model through virtual corrections to SM processes. The $Z'$ boson contributes to electroweak precision observables such as the $S$, $T$, and $U$ parameters measured at LEP and the weak mixing angle $\sin^2\theta_W$, with particularly strong constraints arising from $Z$-pole measurements~\cite{ALEPH:2005ab}. The light scalar $\phi'$ can contribute to the muon anomalous magnetic moment $(g-2)_\mu$ through loop corrections, with the model parameters constrained by the latest experimental measurements~\cite{Zaid:2025dxw}. However, consistency requires that the same couplings responsible for $(g-2)_\mu$ do not generate unacceptably large contributions to electron $g-2$, which is measured with exceptional precision. Rare $B$ and $D$ meson decays at Belle II, LHCb, and future facilities provide sensitivity to $\phi'$ and $Z'$ couplings to quarks through precision measurements of branching ratios and angular distributions. Fixed-target experiments and beam-dump facilities can directly produce light scalars in the sub-GeV to few-GeV mass range if $\phi'$ couples to electrons or light quarks, providing complementary coverage to the LHC strategy presented here. A global fit incorporating all these observables is essential to determine which regions of parameter space remain viable and to identify the most promising signatures for discovery across different experimental platforms.
