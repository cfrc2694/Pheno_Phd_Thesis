
\section{Strategy, Samples and Simulation}\label{sec:sims}


Through the universal see-saw framework, a significant interaction emerges between the top quark and the vector-like quark $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ mediated by the dark Higgs $\phi'$, establishing the $\chi_\mathrm{u} - \mathrm{t} -\phi'$ vertex. This interaction permits $\mathrm{t}\chi_\mathrm{u} \phi'$ final state generation through $\chi_\mathrm{u}$--$\mathrm{t}$ fusion processes, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:qqfusion}. Given that $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ interacts with SM quarks and gluons, abundant production occurs at hadron colliders. The high-energy decay products, combined with a $\phi'$ mediator possessing substantial transverse momentum, enable efficient detection—particularly when $\phi'$ decays yield visible SM particles within the central detector acceptance.

This approach successfully suppresses SM backgrounds while boosting LHC sensitivity to massive top partners and GeV-scale mediators, targets that prove difficult to access through other hadron collider searches. Additionally, $\mathrm{t}\chi_\mathrm{u} \phi'$ final states emerge from $\chi_\mathrm{u}\bar\chi_\mathrm{u}$ pair production through QCD interactions, with one $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ undergoing $\mathrm{t}\phi'$ decay (Fig.~\ref{fig:ggfusion}). The combination of high-energy decay products with a high-$p_T$ mediator yields enhanced sensitivity compared to searches targeting $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ or $\phi'$ independently.

Our study examines scenarios where $\phi'$ exhibits flavor non-universal fermion couplings, following the framework of~\parencite{Dutta2020}. These coupling patterns offer potential explanations for outstanding SM puzzles. The analysis concentrates on $\phi'\to\mu^+\mu^-$ decay channels, exploiting the excellent reconstruction and identification efficiency for muons. This choice enables low-threshold $p_{\mathrm{T}}(\mu)$ triggers while delivering a distinctly clean experimental signature that effectively rejects QCD multijet backgrounds.


The $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model outlined in Sec.~\ref{sec:modelU1} receives \textit{tree-level} implementation within the \texttt{FeynRules} package~\parencite{Alloul:2013bka}, which constructs Feynman rules and outputs them in Universal \texttt{FeynRules} Output (\texttt{UFO}) format~\parencite{Degrande:2011ua}. This \texttt{UFO} serves as generator input for MC sample production, employing the $U(1)_{T^3_R}$ model implementation from Ref.~\parencite{Dutta2023}. Signal and background event generation utilizes \texttt{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO} v3.2.0~\parencite{Alwall:2014hca,Alwall:2014bza} at QCD leading order (LO), modeling \textrm{pp} collisions at center-of-mass energy $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ \textrm{TeV}. Signal and background samples undergo independent generation, with signal-background interference effects neglected. The impact of these interference effects has been evaluated, and for all values of $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ and $\phi'$ masses considered, the effect on the signal plus background cross section is found to be less than $<0.5$\%. Additionally, the effect on the shape of the b-jet $p_{T}$ distribution is less than $6$\% for $p_{T} < 300$ GeV and less than $2$\% for \textrm{b}-jet $p_{T} > 300$ GeV.
Event generation employs \texttt{NNPDF3.0~NLO}~\parencite{NNPDF:2014otw} parton distribution functions (PDFs). Parton-level events subsequently interface with \texttt{PYTHIA} (v8.2.44)~\parencite{Sjostrand:2014zea} for parton shower and hadronization modeling. Detector response simulation, including smearing effects, utilizes \texttt{DELPHES} (v3.4.2)~\parencite{deFavereau:2013fsa} configured with CMS detector geometry and particle identification/reconstruction parameters, assuming $140$ average pileup interactions per event.

Signal cross section calculations apply the following parton-level kinematic requirements within \texttt{MadGraph} for leptons $\ell$, \textrm{b} quarks, and light-quark/gluon jets ($j$): $p_{\mathrm{T}}(\ell) > 35$~\textrm{GeV}, $\abs{\eta (\mathrm{b})} < 2.5$, $\abs{\eta (\ell)} < 2.3$, $p_{\mathrm{T}}(j) > 20$~\textrm{GeV}, and $\abs{\eta (\mathrm{j})} < 5$. These parton-level constraints apply solely to signal generation, confining event production to relevant phase space regions. Background generation employs these parton-level thresholds in \texttt{MadGraph}: $p_{\mathrm{T}}(\ell) > 10$~\textrm{GeV}, $\abs{\eta (\ell)} < 2.5$, $p_{\mathrm{T}}(j) > 20$~\textrm{GeV}, $\abs{\eta (\mathrm{j})} < 5$, and $\abs{\eta (\mathrm{b})} < 5$. This strategy ensures adequate phase space coverage for backgrounds following parton showering, given that analysis-level pre-selections impose tighter criteria than parton-level cuts. Jet matching and merging employ the MLM algorithm with parameters \texttt{xqcut}$=30$ and \texttt{qcut}$=45$, selected to maintain differential jet rate continuity across jet multiplicities. Each sample generates one million LO events independently, omitting signal-background interference due to substantial coupling constant hierarchy suppression.

Signal generation targets $\phi'$ boson production alongside $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ vector-like quark and top quark final states $(\mathrm{pp}\to \chi_\mathrm{u} \mathrm{t} \phi')$ (Figures~\ref{fig:qqfusion}-\ref{fig:ggfusion}). The $\mathrm{pp}\to \chi_\mathrm{u} \mathrm{t} \phi'$ production cross section exhibits strong sensitivity to Lagrangian Yukawa coupling choices. The ${\chi_\mathrm{u}}{- \mathrm{t}}$ fusion mechanism (Fig.~\ref{fig:qqfusion}) depends predominantly on $Y_{\mathrm{t}_R}$ coupling strength. Conversely, the ${\chi_\mathrm{u}} \to \mathrm{t} \phi'$ decay process (Fig.~\ref{fig:ggfusion}) scales inversely with $Y_{\mathrm{t}_L}$ coupling. Fig.~\ref{fig:cross_section_by_lambdas} illustrates this behavior, presenting total signal cross section as a function of both $Y_{\mathrm{t}_R}$ and $Y_{\mathrm{t}_L}$ at the benchmark configuration $m_{\phi'}=100$~\textrm{GeV}, $m_{\chi_\mathrm{u}}=1.0$~\textrm{TeV}.

Consequently, we adopt a benchmark point with coupling parameters $Y_{\mathrm{t}_R}=Y_{\mathrm{t}_L}=2\sqrt{2}$, scanning scalar masses $m_{\phi'}\in\{5,\, 10,\, 50,\, 100,\, 325\}$~\textrm{GeV} and vector-like quark masses $m_{\chi_\mathrm{u}}\in\{0.50,\, 0.75,\, 1.0,\, 1.5,\, 2.0,\, 2.5\}$~\textrm{TeV}~\parencite{PhysRevD.108.095006}. Signal event topologies feature hadronic top decay to bottom quark plus dijet system ($\mathrm{t} \to \mathrm{bW} \to \mathrm{b} q \bar{q}'$), exploiting its favorable branching fraction of $\approx 67.2$\%. Additionally, $\chi_\mathrm{u}$ undergoes semi-leptonic decay producing $b$ quark, lepton, and neutrino (through $\chi_\mathrm{u} \to \mathrm{bW}$ followed by $\mathrm{W}\to\mu\nu_{\mu}$), while $\phi'$ decays to muon pairs. These channels yield clean experimental signatures accessible with modest trigger $p_T$ requirements. We note that the scalar $\phi'$ may arise from SM Higgs mixing with additional scalar sectors, with fermion Yukawas potentially originating from SM-fermion mixing with vector-like fermion replicas. Consequently, $\phi'$ branching fractions depend on the specific mixing mechanism and model implementation, as explored in Refs.~\parencite{Cacciapaglia_2023,Blankenburg:2012nx,Jones-Perez:2013oia,Calibbi:2009pv}. Following Refs.~\parencite{Dutta2020,Dutta2023}, our benchmark scenarios assume $\mathfrak{B}(\chi_\mathrm{u} \rightarrow \textrm{b W})\approx 0.5$ and $\mathfrak{B}(\phi' \rightarrow \mu^+\mu^-)=1.00$. Fig.~\ref{fig:xs-plot} presents production cross sections (\textrm{fb}) across the $m_{\phi'}$-$m_{\chi_\mathrm{u}}$ mass plane under these decay assumptions, branching fractions, and coupling choices.

Within the parameter space examined here, the combined $\mathrm{t}$-$\chi_\mathrm{u}$ system mass exceeds $m_{\phi'}$, enabling conversion of substantial rest energy into large $\phi'$ momentum. Similarly, top quarks from $\chi_\mathrm{u}$-$t$ fusion acquire high momentum, occasionally preventing independent reconstruction of hadronic top decay products. This kinematic regime produces three distinct top reconstruction topologies: fully merged, where highly collimated $\mathrm{W}\to jj$ and $\mathrm{b}$ systems form a single fat jet (FatJet, FJ); partially merged, where $\mathrm{W}$ decay products coalesce into one FatJet while the $\mathrm{b}$ quark remains resolved; and unmerged, where all decay products receive independent identification. Jet clustering employs the anti-$k_t$ algorithm~\parencite{Cacciari_2008} via \texttt{FastJet} (v3.4.2)~\parencite{Cacciari_2012}, using distance parameter $R = 0.4$ for standard jets and $R = 0.8$ for fat jets. Each topology carries characteristic identification efficiency and misidentification probability, dependent on the boosted $\mathrm{t/W}$ tagging algorithm selection (efficiency and misidentification specifications appear below). 

Given these considerations, the targeted final state comprises three muons (two originating from $\phi'$, one from $\chi_\mathrm{u}$), a top-tagged system (potentially boosted), at minimum one $\mathrm{b}$-tagged jet, and substantial $\vec{p}_{T}^{\textrm{~miss}}$. Partially merged and unmerged topologies yield two $\mathrm{b}$ quarks in the final state (with one incorporated within the top-tagged system). 

Background contributions arise from SM processes producing final-state objects mimicking signal signatures. Examined background sources include QCD multijet production, diboson processes ($\mathrm{VV: WW, ZZ, WZ}$), triboson events ($\mathrm{VVV: WWZ, WZZ, ZZZ, WWW}$), top pair production with weak bosons ($\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}X$), and four-top production ($\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}$). Primary background contributions originate from $\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}X$, $\mathrm{ZZW}$, and $\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}$ processes. The $\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}X$ background predominantly consists of $\mathrm{Z}/\gamma^{*}$ associated production through $\mathrm{t}\bar{\mathrm{t}}$ fusion. The $\mathrm{ZZW}$ channel contributes background when one $\mathrm{Z}$ yields $\mathrm{b}\bar{\mathrm{b}}$, the second $\mathrm{Z}$ produces muon pairs, and W decays leptonically to muon-neutrino.
Cross-section-weighted combinations of $\mathrm{ZZW}$ and $\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}$ events constitute the ``$\mathrm{b} \overline{\mathrm{b}}\mu\mu\mu\nu$'' background category hereafter. The $\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}X$ process comprises the ``$\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}$'' background classification. Fig.~\ref{fig:v} illustrates a representative $\mathrm{t}\overline{\mathrm{t}}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}$ background diagram. Tab.~\ref{tab:dominantbkgs} lists dominant background production cross sections. Remaining background processes contribute negligibly, accounting for $\ll 1\%$ of total expected background.

Lepton, boosted top quark, and bottom quark identification critically determines signal event recognition capability, SM background suppression, and consequently LHC discovery potential in high-luminosity conditions. Lepton and top/bottom quark decay product reconstruction presents significant challenges at the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) given substantial in-time and out-of-time pileup (PU) interaction contamination. Pileup impact on new physics discovery sensitivity and mitigation importance for CMS and ATLAS receives extensive discussion in the literature, exemplified by Ref.~\parencite{CMS-PAS-FTR-13-014}. While anticipated upgraded detector performance at HL-LHC exceeds this work's scope, conservative efficiency degradation assumptions provide realistic projections. Following Ref.~\parencite{CMS-PAS-FTR-13-014} benchmarks, simulations incorporate $140$ average PU interactions via \texttt{DELPHES} configuration, as previously described. 

Muon identification in the central region ($|\eta|< 1.5$) assumes $95$\% efficiency with $0.3$\% misidentification probability~\parencite{CMS-PAS-FTR-13-014,CMS_MUON_17001}. Performance deteriorates linearly across $1.5 < |\eta| < 2.5$, reaching $65$\% efficiency and $0.5$\% misidentification at $|\eta| = 2.5$. Charged hadron tracking efficiency—relevant for jet clustering and $\vec{p}_{T}^{\textrm{~miss}}$ computation—equals $97$\% throughout $1.5 < |\eta| < 2.5$, declining to $85$\% at $|\eta| = 2.5$. These pileup-induced inefficiencies affect lepton and top kinematic reconstruction quality. Following Refs.~\parencite{CMS:2020poo,ATLAS:2018wis}, the ``Loose'' operating point identifies fully merged (partially merged) top decays with $80-85$\% top (W) efficiency and $11-25$\% misidentification, varying with FatJet transverse momentum ($p_{T}^{FJ}$). Per Ref.~\parencite{CMSbtag}, the DeepCSV algorithm~\parencite{Bols_2020} ``Loose'' working point delivers $70-80$\% b-tagging efficiency alongside $10$\% light-flavor misidentification. Boosted top/W and b-tagging operating point selection emerges from discovery reach optimization. SM background contribution from misidentified boosted top/W proves negligible, rendering discovery projections insensitive to boosted top/W misidentification uncertainties.
