\section{Current Exclusion Limits on Vector-Like Quarks}\label{sec:exp}

Extensive experimental efforts by the ATLAS and CMS collaborations at CERN have targeted the detection of heavy vector-like quarks, among others. These investigations have employed proton-proton collisions at center-of-mass energies of $\sqrt{s} = 8$ and $13$ \textrm{TeV}, examining $\chi_u$ decay modes into $\mathrm{bW}$, $\mathrm{tZ}$, and $\mathrm{tH}$ final states. The primary focus has been on $\chi_u$ generation via QCD interactions, encompassing both pair creation through quark-antiquark annihilation (Fig.~\ref{fig:qcd_T_prod}) and single-$\chi_u$ production through electroweak interactions with accompanying quarks (Fig.~\ref{fig:qed_T_prod}).

$\chi_u$ branching fractions depends on the specific theoretical framework. For QCD-mediated $\chi_u$ pair creation ($\chi_u\bar{\chi}_u$), the production cross sections depend exclusively on the vector-like quark mass. Under the assumptions of a narrow decay width ($\Gamma / m(\chi_u) < 0.05$ or $0.1$) and exclusive branching to either $\textrm{bW}$, $\textrm{tZ}$, or $\textrm{tH}$, these experimental searches have established robust constraints on $m(\chi_u)$, ruling out masses up to approximately $1.5$ \textrm{TeV} at the $95$\% confidence level~\parencite{CMS:2024bni,CMS:2024qdd,ATLAS:2022ozf,ATLAS:2023bfh,ATLAS:2022hnn,ATLAS:2022tla,ATLAS:2023pja,ATLAS:2024fdw}. 

Recent ATLAS investigations target vector-like quarks through several complementary channels: single-$\chi_u$ generation with $\chi_u \to \textrm{tH}$ yielding fully hadronic signatures~\parencite{ATLAS:2022ozf}, single-$\chi_u$ generation with $\chi_u \to \textrm{tZ}$ producing multilepton signatures~\parencite{ATLAS:2023bfh}, $\chi_u\chi_u$ pair generation with multiple decay modes resulting in multilepton signatures~\parencite{ATLAS:2022hnn}, and $\chi_u\chi_u$ pair generation with various decay channels producing single-lepton plus missing momentum signatures~\parencite{ATLAS:2022tla,ATLAS:2023pja}. The multilepton channel provides superior sensitivity across most of the parameter space, whereas the missing transverse energy approach exhibits enhanced sensitivity when $\mathfrak{B}(\chi_u\to \textrm{Wb})$ is small and $\mathfrak{B}(\chi_u\to \textrm{Ht})$ is large. Both singlet and doublet scenarios show comparable experimental sensitivities, with mass exclusions extending to roughly $1.25$ \textrm{TeV} and $1.41$ \textrm{TeV}, respectively. The latest CMS investigation examines $\chi_u$-quark generation through $\mathrm{pp} \to \chi_u\textrm{qb}$, targeting final states containing $\chi_u \to \textrm{tZ}$ or $\chi_u \to \textrm{tH}$ transitions, with emphasis on scenarios featuring enhanced couplings to third-generation quarks. This study establishes upper bounds at the $95$\% confidence level ranging from $68$ to $1260$ \textrm{fb} on the production cross section for $\chi_u$ masses between 600 and 1200 \textrm{GeV}~\parencite{CMS:2024qdd}.

The mass exclusion range becomes less stringent for certain branching fraction combinations, such as $\{\mathfrak{B}(\chi_u \to \textrm{tZ})$, $\mathfrak{B}(\chi_u \to bW)$, $\mathfrak{B}(\chi_u \to \textrm{tH})\}= \{0.2, 0.6, 0.2\}$, which constrains masses only below approximately $0.95$ \textrm{TeV}. Additionally, when the $\chi_u \to \phi't $ decay channel becomes accessible, or when the branching fractions $\mathfrak{B}(\chi_u \to \textrm{tH/bW})$ are reduced, the aforementioned exclusion limits require reassessment. As highlighted in Ref.~\parencite{Cacciapaglia:2019zmj}, the constraints on $m(\chi_u)$ may be relaxed to approximately $500$ \textrm{GeV} when $\chi_u \to \mathrm{t}\phi'$ transitions are kinematically allowed. Therefore, to facilitate a comprehensive study, benchmark scenarios in this paper are considered down to $m(\chi_\mathrm{u}) = 500$ \textrm{GeV}.
